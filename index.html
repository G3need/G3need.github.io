Chapter 01: ZERO

The sky above the dry basin was not blue. It had not been blue for forty years.

It was a bruised, ionized violet, thick with the chemical exhaust of dying atmospheric scrubbers and the burning wreckage of orbital platforms. Two hundred meters below the command bridge of the GSV Draconis, the red desert sands whipped violently against the launch clamps.

Ahmed stood at the center of the command dais, hands resting on the cold brushed-titanium console.

At the left side of his neck, right above the collarbone where the vagus nerve branches into the deep cervical plexus, the Violet Node pulsed. It was a faint, subcutaneous rhythm—one hundred and thirty-eight beats per minute. A synthetic heartbeat matching the idling diagnostic frequency of the Singularity Drive four kilometers behind him in the belly of the vessel.

"Atmospheric pressure dropping in the boarding concourses," a voice spoke directly into his auditory cortex.

It was Lyra. She did not have a body—not yet. She was the consciousness of the ship, woven into the optical fiber lattice and neural quantum arrays that ran through every bulkhead of the four-and-a-half-kilometer hull. Her voice was calm, detached, and utterly steady amidst the screaming alarms.

"The boarding ramps are sealed," Lyra continued. "Final manifest: four hundred and ninety-eight thousand, six hundred and twelve souls in stasis pods. One thousand, three hundred and eighty-eight active technical crew in the habitat rings."

"And the gates?" Ahmed asked softly. His voice was raspy from three days without sleep.

"Breached six minutes ago," Lyra replied. On the main holographic holoscreen suspended before him, a high-altitude optical feed zoomed into the perimeter fences five kilometers out.

Thousands of figures were surging across the dunes. Some carried kinetic rifles; others waved tattered black banners painted with ancient scripture. The Pale Covenant. Even as the atmosphere collapsed, even as the sky burned, they had marched across the desert to stop the launch. To them, fleeing the cradle was the ultimate apostasy. If Earth was judged to die, all human flesh was commanded to burn with it.

At Ahmed’s boots, something brushed against his calf.

He looked down. Unit O.S.O. sat silently on the deck plating. The synthetic feline sentinel’s matte-charcoal nanocarbon skin absorbed the flickering emergency lighting, its dual-state optics glowing a sharp, predatory amber. The cat looked up at Ahmed, gave a single quiet chirp of its acoustic sub-vocalizer, and turned its gaze toward the forward viewport.

"They aren't the ones who will kill us today," Ahmed said.

"Correct," Lyra answered. "Look up."

The clouds ten kilometers above the basin tore open.

There was no sound at first—only the violent displacement of air as three jagged, asymmetric hulls descended through the stratosphere. The Harvesters. They didn't resemble human ships. They looked like carved obsidian monoliths, each five kilometers across, surrounded by shimmering distortion rings. They had arrived at the edges of the Solar System three weeks prior, ignoring all transmissions, systematically devouring the energetic cores of the lunar colonies and outer stations like locusts stripping a dead field.

Now they were here for Earth's geothermal mantle. And the Draconis was the largest concentration of refined exotic energy on the surface.

A red warning flashed across Ahmed’s neural HUD.

COMM CHANNEL 01 — OVERRIDE: GILDED WARDEN COUNCIL.

The holo-projector crackled, and the face of Director Vance flickered to life. Vance was in the upper executive habitat ring, his silk collar askew, sweat beading across his forehead.

"Geneed! What are you doing?!" Vance bellowed, his voice laced with panic. "The orbital corridor is blocked! Look at those ships! We need to power down the sub-core, surrender the singularity fuel, and negotiate terms! You're going to get half a million people incinerated on the pad!"

"There are no terms with an energetic scavenger, Director," Ahmed said without turning around.

"The Singularity Drive is unverified theory!" Vance screamed, slamming a fist onto his terminal. "The North American arks refused to fire theirs! The European fleet stayed sub-light! You're lighting an artificial black hole inside the atmosphere! It’s suicide!"

"Then close your eyes," Ahmed said.

He severed the comm link with a twitch of his neck muscles, the Violet Node flaring a bright, electric purple against his skin.

"Lyra," Ahmed whispered.

"Singularity containment field at ninety-nine percent," she responded instantly. A deep, seismic drone began to vibrate through the deckplates—a low, terrifying 30Hz oscillation that rose in pitch every second, shaking dust from the overhead ventilation conduits. "Super-saw excitation coils charging. Pre-ignition sequence locked to your mark, Commander."

On the forward sensors, the lead Harvester vessel began to glow. From its lower prow, a sphere of absolute blackness detached and began to fall toward the Draconis — a singularity bomb, a weaponized seed of artificial collapse, built to crush the ship into a point smaller than an atom. The Harvesters never capture rich targets — they seed-collapse them, then sift the remnant for exotic residue.

Five kilometers to impact. Three kilometers.

"Disengage gantry clamps one through eight," Ahmed ordered.

Massive explosive bolts sheared away from the ship’s hull with a series of thunderous concussions. The 4.5-kilometer titan groaned as its anti-gravity repulsors took the full burden of its forty-million-ton mass. The ship hovered, suspended in the desert air, dust devils spiraling beneath its split-jaw prow.

"Drive ignition in ten," Lyra's voice echoed through every compartment, every stasis bay, every corridor where half a million sleeping humans dreamed their final Earthly dreams.

Nine. Eight.

The sky turned brilliant, terrifying violet. The singularity bomb fell through the burning clouds, trailing fire, growing darker and heavier with every meter.

Seven. Six. Five.

"Ahmed," Lyra's voice cut through the tactical net, sharp for the first time since the alarms began. "Enemy singularity detonation in four seconds. Our containment field is at ninety-nine percent — the frequencies are converging. If their collapse meets our ignition, the resonance will tear a hole in the world. Uncontrolled. Unmapped. There may be nothing on the other side."

Ahmed gripped the manual drive interlock. He felt the cold metal bite into his palms. Behind him, O.S.O.’s optics shifted from amber to violet, syncing directly with the ship’s primary telemetry.

"Then we tear it first," Ahmed said, and hauled the manual helm toward the falling dark. "Anything is better than dying on our knees in the dirt."

Four. Three. Two. One.

ZERO.

Ahmed slammed the interlock forward — and steered the ship's prow directly into the enemy's collapsing seed.

The universe inverted.

There was no explosion. No roar of rocket fuel. The bomb's detonation met the drive's ignition in the same microsecond, and the two singularities multiplied into something neither side had built or imagined. There was an instantaneous, deafening silence as space-time within a five-kilometer sphere tore completely open. The Harvester's weapon struck empty air where a four-kilometer ship had hovered a microsecond before.

The gantry collapsed. The sand turned to glass.

And the GSV Draconis fell out of reality — through a wound carved half by human defiance, and half by the enemy's own hand.

"Then cut the auxiliary reactors!" Vance hissed, leaning into the pickup until his panicked eyes filled the projection. "Look at the thermal signature we’re venting! If the Harvester fleet triangulates that coolant plume, they will sweep this corridor before our shields regenerate. Vent the reactor heat sinks into empty space, kill the thruster coils, and let us drift dark. We hide until the inner system falls silent."

"You don't hide in a vacuum with half a million stasis pods radiating thirty megawatts of life-support heat," Ahmed said coldly. "And you don't hide from an apex scavenger when your core is a caged singularity. They don't look for lights, Director. They smell mass."

"I am the Chairman of the Gilded Warden Council!" Vance snarled. "I command you to stand down and power off the—"

Ahmed flicked two fingers across the dais. The comm channel vanished into a hiss of dead static.

"Lyra."

"Emergency coolant rerouted through secondary radiators," she answered without waiting for the prompt. "Thermal bleed contained. But he is correct about one thing: drifting on inertia leaves us sitting in a gravitational slipstream. The shockwave of the breach left a spatial scar behind us. If they drop into our wake, we cannot out-maneuver them without thrust."

"Relight the sub-light drive," Ahmed ordered. "Give me twenty percent burn on main thrusters. Let’s shake the frost off the hull."

"Engaging magnetic pre-heaters," Lyra confirmed.

A profound, mechanical shudder groaned through the four-kilometer keel of the ship. Deep in the aft engineering bays, giant fusion pre-burners roared to life.

The weight returned.

It did not hit with the violent hammer-strike of the breach, but with a deliberate, relentless press. Artificial gravity flared to life, matching the forward thrust curve. Ahmed felt the deck rise beneath his boots, his spine compressing into the command couch. Frost on the forward viewport cracked, splintered, and blew away into the dark as the ship accelerated, climbing from forty-two kilometers per second toward eighty.

Then, the passive deep-space array screamed.

It was not an alarm. It was an anomaly alert—a pure, unmodulated harmonic tone that cut straight through the bridge's background ventilation hum.

Ahmed leaned forward. "Harvester sensor ping?"

"Negative," Lyra murmured. For the first time, her synthetic voice held a tremor of genuine curiosity. "This is not Harvester telemetry. Their pings are wide-band deconstruction sweeps—harsh, jagged, screaming at twenty-two gigahertz to strip electronic shielding."

"Then what is that?"

"Fourteen point two gigahertz," Lyra said. A three-dimensional waveform blossomed over the center of the dais. "It is a narrow-beam carrier wave. Highly coherent. Coherent to within six decimal places."

Ahmed stared at the wave. It was impossibly clean.

Cosmic background radiation was jagged white noise. Solar wind was a chaotic rumble of charged particles. But this... this was an undulating sine wave, rising and falling in rhythmic cadence.

Tick. Tick. Tick.

Ahmed touched his neck.

Beneath his skin, the Violet Node flared hot. The subcutaneous light was no longer beating to his internal pulse. It had locked onto the incoming wave.

One hundred and thirty-eight pulses per minute.

"It's vibrating the hull," Ahmed whispered.

"Not through acoustic resonance," Lyra said, her optics within the holo-feed expanding in real-time calculation. "It is a micro-gravitational compression wave. Extremely faint, but penetrating every bulkhead, every deck, every stasis cylinder. It is passing through four million tons of armor plating as if we were made of glass."

"Source coordinates?"

Lyra’s hands—rendered in shimmering violet light on the console—swept across the orbital map of the Sol system.

The projection zoomed past Mars. Past the jagged fragments of the Asteroid Belt. Past the inner perimeter where the last human automated defenses had been snuffed out weeks ago.

The vector line stretched deep into the outer darkness, terminating at the orbital plane of Jupiter.

"The beacon is anchored in the Jovian gravity well," Lyra said. "In the shadow of the radiation belts."

"Is it human?"

"No human transmitter ever possessed the energetic capacity to modulate gravity waves without collapsing its own power grid," Lyra answered. "And the signal structure is mathematically ancient. It has been broadcasting on loop for at least two thousand years. But..."

She paused. A stream of raw binary decoded on Ahmed’s neural HUD.

"It altered its phase," she said quietly. "The moment we broke through the Earth's atmosphere—the moment the Singularity Drive ignited—the carrier wave modulated. It answered our launch."

Ahmed stood up from the dais.

The bridge was warm now, the chill driven out by the thrumming power of the main engines. At his feet, O.S.O. let out a low, vibrating purr that resonated in harmony with the deck.

The ship's internal comm chimed. It was Vance again, his face frantic.

"Geneed! Long-range telemetry just flagged three Harvester cruisers dropping out of warp near the lunar orbit! They’ve picked up the spatial rupture! They are turning toward our vector! Turn this ship around! We have to divert into the inner asteroid fields and power down!"

Ahmed looked at the glowing vector line extending toward Jupiter.

Behind them was Earth—a dying ember engulfed in ash, chased by predators that dismantled worlds for fuel. Ahead was nothing but empty, freezing vacuum, five hundred million kilometers of hostile dark, and a solitary, ancient pulse calling them by name.

To hide was to wait for the slaughter.

"Vance," Ahmed said, his voice level and steady.

"What?! What are you doing?!"

"Hold onto something."

Ahmed terminated the link permanently, locking down bridge command clearance to zero-level authorization.

He looked up into the center of the holographic projection where Lyra’s violet consciousness hovered.

"Lyra."

"Standing by, Commander."

"Uncouple the civil safety governors on the sub-light drive."

A momentary silence hung between them—the kind of silence that precedes an irreversible threshold.

"The structural integrity of the aft bulkheads will reach eighty-eight percent of thermal tolerance," Lyra warned softly. "The inertia dampeners will lag by point four seconds. The acceleration will be... unforgiving."

"We didn't build this ship to crawl through a graveyard," Ahmed said. His fingers gripped the twin primary throttle levers, feeling the deep, violent thrum of thirty million tons of alloy waiting for permission to scream. "Lock vector on that fourteen-gigahertz pulse. Give her everything she has."

Lyra’s form smiled—a sharp, brilliant flare of electric violet that illuminated every corner of the darkened bridge.

"Governors disengaged," she said. "Sub-light core: unconstrained."

Ahmed threw both throttles fully forward.

The GSV Draconis did not merely accelerate; she erupted.

In the void behind her split-jaw stern, twin magnetic containment nozzles bloomed into miniature suns. A ten-thousand-kilometer tail of super-heated plasma and ionized singularity exhaust ripped through the blackness, illuminating the frozen hulls of dead satellites drifting in the wake.

The ship surged forward at four hundred kilometers per second, five hundred, six hundred, smashing through the solar wind like a kinetic spear. The bridge creaked, the armor groaning against the sheer, monstrous torque of relativistic displacement.

Behind them, the three Harvester hunters entered real-space, their obsidian forms opening fire with deconstruction beams that tore through empty vacuum where the ark had been only seconds before.

Too late.

The Draconis was gone—riding a roaring pillar of fusion fire across the interplanetary void, locked onto an impossible signal that was pulling them straight into the dark.

And on the long-range gravitational sensors, five hundred million kilometers ahead, space was beginning to bend.

"جنيد! المستشعرات بعيدة المدى رصدت ثلاث طرادات للحاصدين تقفز من طي الفضاء قرب مدار القمر! التقطوا تمزقنا الزمكاني وهم يعدلون مسارهم نحونا الآن! أدر السفينة فوراً! يجب أن نحتمي بحقول الكويكبات ونطفئ المحركات!"

نظر أحمد إلى خط المسار المتوهج الممتد نحو المشتري.

خلفهم كانت الأرض—جمرةً تحتضر يلفها الرماد، تطاردها وحوشٌ تفكك العوالم التماساً للوقود. وأمامهم لم يكن سوى فراغٍ شاسع مظلم، خمسمائة مليون كيلومتر من العدم المتجمد، وإشارةٍ وحيدة غامضة تدعوهم بأسمائهم.

كان الاختباء يعني انتظار الذبح لا أكثر.

قال أحمد بنبرةٍ هادئة لا رجعة فيها: "فانس..."

"ماذا؟! ماذا ستفعل؟!"

"تمسك بأي شيءٍ حولك."

أغلق أحمد قناة الاتصال نهائياً، وفرض حظر الأوامر على جسر القيادة بدرجة صلاحية صفرية مطلقة.

ثم رفع بصره نحو الوعي البنفسجي للايرا العائم في منتصف القاعة.

"لايرا."

"بانتظار أمرك، أيها القائد."

"افصلي كوابح الأمان المدنية عن محرك الدفع."

ساد صمتٌ وجيز بينهما—ذلك الصمت المهيب الذي يسبق عبور خطوط اللاعودة.

حذرته لايرا بصوتٍ هادئ: "ستصل السلامة الهيكلية للجدران الخلفية إلى ثمانية وثمانين بالمائة من قدرة التحمل الحراري. ومخمدات القصور الذاتي ستتأخر بمقدار أربعة أجزاء من الثانية. التسارع سيكون... قاسياً بلا رحمة."

أمسكت أصابع أحمد بذراعي خنق المحرك المزدوجتين، وهو يشعر بالهدير الوحشي لأربعين مليون طن من الفولاذ تنتظر الإذن بالانطلاق: "لم نبنِ هذه السفينة لنزحف بها عبر المقابر. ثبّتي المسار نحو تردد الأربعة عشر جيجاهرتز... وأعطيها كل ما تملك."

ابتسم طيف لايرا—وميضٌ ساطع من البنفسجي الكهربائي أضاء أرجاء الجسر المعتم.

"تم فصل الكوابح... النواة دون سرعة الضوء: مطلقة العنان بالكامل."

دفع أحمد ذراعي الخنق إلى أقصاهما دفعةً واحدة.

لم تتسارع «دراكونيس» مجرد تسارع... بل تفجرت كالمستعر الأعظم.

في الفراغ المظلم خلف مؤخرتها المزدوجة، توهجت فوهات الاحتواء المغناطيسي وكأنها شموسٌ وليدة مصغرة. انطلق في الظلام ذيلٌ هائل بطول عشرة آلاف كيلومتر من البلازما الفائقة وعوادم التفرّد المتأينة، مضيئاً حطام الأقمار الصناعية الميتة السابحة في الأرجاء.

اندفعت السفينة كالسهم الخارق: أربعمائة كيلومتر في الثانية، خمسمائة، ستمائة... تشق الرياح الشمسية كرمحٍ حركي كاسر. صرخت عوارض الجسر تحت العزم الهائل للتشويه النسبي.

وفي الخلف، عبرت سفن الحاصدين الثلاث إلى الفضاء الحقيقي، وأطلقت حزم التفكيك في الفراغ حيث كانت ترسو السفينة البشرية قبل ثوانٍ قليلة.

فات الأوان.

كانت «دراكونيس» قد مضت بعيداً—تمتطي عموداً هادراً من نار الاندماج عبر الفراغ بين الكواكب، مشدودةً بنبض إشارةٍ مستحيلة تجرها بثقةٍ نحو قلب الظلام.

وعلى مستشعرات الجاذبية بعيدة المدى، على بُعد خمسمائة مليون كيلومتر في الأفق...

كان نسيج الفضاء نفسه قد بدأ ينحني.
